  CONCEPT SUMMARY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

A first-person horror game heavily inspired by Chilla's Art, set across the
reading week before a university student's final exam. The player must balance
four life pillars — School Work, Socializing, Daily Chores, and Entertainment —
while managing a rising Stress & Anxiety meter as exam day closes in. The
apartment is haunted, and paranormal activity bleeds into every mundane task.
The horror never stops the game — it happens AROUND the player while they work,
in the style of Mortuary Assistant: something moves in the corner while you
study; something is in the hallway while you cook. You can investigate or
ignore it, but the tasks don't pause.

The game spans exactly 7 days (Reading Week Mon → Sun) plus the Exam on Monday.

Locations:
  - The Apartment  (primary hub — all daily life and paranormal activity)
  - The University Campus  (Day 1 orientation + Exam Day only)

The tone is lo-fi, oppressive, and mundane-turned-terrifying — the horror
comes from recognizable student anxiety made literal.

  CORE PILLARS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. SCHOOL WORK
   - Interactable computer in the apartment for online study
   - In-person lectures and an exam at the university campus
   - Exam difficulty scales with current Stress Level (harder under pressure)

2. SOCIALIZING
   - "Discord" voice call overlay — stylized UI, pre-written dialogue choices
   - On-campus NPC interactions (friend, professor)
   - Skipping social tasks builds stress

3. DAILY CHORES
   - Cooking (stove + fridge interaction)
   - Washing dishes (sink)
   - Laundry (washer → dryer, timed)
   - Feeding the cat
   - Taking out the garbage (carry bag to exit)
   - Working out (push-ups / sit-ups — button rhythm mechanic on the apartment floor)

4. ENTERTAINMENT
   - Computer gaming: a Flappy Bird-style minigame playable on the in-game PC
   - Provides the most stress relief but costs the most time

5. STRESS & ANXIETY (the spine of the game)
   - A single float value from 0 to 100
   - Rises from: incomplete daily tasks, failing the exam, skipping meals/sleep
   - Lowers from: completing tasks, gaming, socializing, working out
   - Drives ALL paranormal activity (see Paranormal System below)
   - Visualized via a physical whiteboard in the apartment

  PARANORMAL SYSTEM
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Design approach: HYBRID (dynamic stress-driven tiers + authored day-floor minimums)

  Dynamic: paranormal tier is determined by current Stress Level in real time.
  Authored: each day has a minimum stress floor, ensuring pacing even if the
            player is managing their tasks well.

  Stress Tier Table:
  ┌──────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  0  – 25     │  TIER 1 — Subtle                                            │
  │              │  Flickering lights, faint sounds at edge of hearing         │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │  25 – 50     │  TIER 2 — Moderate                                          │
  │              │  Doors opening alone, cat staring at nothing,               │
  │              │  silhouette in window reflection                            │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │  50 – 75     │  TIER 3 — Active                                            │
  │              │  Figure at end of hallway that vanishes, phone calls,       │
  │              │  objects found misplaced                                    │
  ├──────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │  75 – 100    │  TIER 4 — Confrontational                                   │
  │              │  Jumpscares, entity visible in the apartment                │
  └──────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  Day Floor Minimums — Reading Week progression:
    Day 1  Mon  → Floor 0   (clean slate, paranormal entirely avoidable)
    Day 2  Tue  → Floor 10  (first hint — one guaranteed Tier 1 event)
    Day 3  Wed  → Floor 25  (Tier 1 always active, apartment feels wrong)
    Day 4  Thu  → Floor 35  (Tier 2 bleeds in, familiar spaces feel hostile)
    Day 5  Fri  → Floor 50  (Tier 2 guaranteed, harder to ignore)
    Day 6  Sat  → Floor 60  (Tier 3 active, no safe room in the apartment)
    Day 7  Sun  → Floor 70  (Exam Eve — Tier 3/4 boundary, dread peaks)
    Exam   Mon  → Floor 80  (Tier 4 — player carries everything into the exam)

  ALARM CLOCK MECHANIC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Before the player sleeps, they must interact with the alarm clock on the
  nightstand and set a wake-up time. The time chosen is a direct tradeoff:

    Earlier alarm  → more day time, but harder wake-up minigame (groggier)
    Later alarm    → easier wake-up, but less time for tasks (stress penalty risk)

  WAKE-UP MINIGAME
  When the alarm fires, the screen fades in blurry and disorienting. The player
  must complete a short minigame to get out of bed. Failing it hits snooze —
  the alarm fires again after a short in-game delay, costing time.

  Minigame design (pick one per day, rotate to avoid repetition):
    BUTTON MASH       Rapidly press E to silence the alarm before it runs out.
                      Sleep-deprived = fewer button presses allowed.
    FOCUS             A blurry crosshair must be steadied over a small target.
                      Sways wildly on high-stress days.
    SEQUENCE          A short Simon-style button sequence shown briefly then
                      hidden. Gets longer as the week progresses.

  Snooze logic:
    - Each snooze costs a fixed block of in-game time (e.g. 30 min)
    - 3 snoozes in a row applies a small stress penalty ("overslept again")
    - On Exam Day, snoozing too many times locks the player out of prep time
      and feeds directly into exam difficulty

  AlarmClockManager owns:
    - SetAlarm(hour)         called when player interacts with clock before sleep
    - OnAlarmFired()         triggers wake-up sequence + minigame
    - Snooze()               delays next alarm, increments snooze counter
    - WakeUp()               clears alarm state, begins new day, updates DaySystem

  Horror tie-in:
    The alarm going off is one of the few guaranteed moments the player is
    disoriented and helpless. On high-stress days (Day 5+), a paranormal event
    can fire DURING the wake-up minigame — a figure at the doorway while the
    screen is still blurry, a sound from under the bed before the player can
    move. The player cannot act on it yet. That helplessness is the point.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
  MORTUARY ASSISTANT-STYLE HORROR
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  Core principle borrowed from Mortuary Assistant: the horror happens AROUND
  the player while they are doing something else. The task never pauses. The
  player chooses whether to investigate or push through — both choices have cost.

  This reframes the paranormal system entirely. Events do not interrupt gameplay.
  They bleed into it.

  EXAMPLES BY TASK:

    STUDYING AT COMPUTER
      Something moves in the monitor reflection behind you.
      A new browser tab opens by itself while you're reading notes.
      Your study material text briefly scrambles and re-sets.
      A knock on the front door — no one is there if you check.

    COOKING
      A figure is visible at the end of the hallway through the kitchen doorway.
      The cat bolts from the room for no reason mid-cook.
      The stove pilot light goes out on its own.
      You hear your own name spoken quietly from another room.

    DOING LAUNDRY
      The washing machine starts a cycle you didn't start.
      Something scrapes along the inside of the dryer while it runs.
      The hallway light outside the laundry area flickers and dies.

    WORKING OUT
      Counts on screen tick backwards briefly.
      A shadow passes under the gap of the closed bedroom door.
      The room temperature visually "drops" (breath fog, slight blue grade).

    TRYING TO SLEEP
      Lying in bed, unable to sleep — sounds from the kitchen.
      The room is dark but something is briefly visible in the doorway.
      Phone buzzes with an unknown contact, no message — just open air.

  INVESTIGATE OR IGNORE — the player's dilemma:
    Investigating costs task time → risks incomplete tasks → raises stress
    Ignoring builds psychological pressure → the paranormal escalates faster
                                             if never acknowledged
    This is the core tension loop. Neither choice is safe.

  IMPLEMENTATION NOTES:
    - All paranormal events during tasks are flagged as "ambient" vs "blocking"
      Ambient = player can see/hear but keep doing the task (most events)
      Blocking = rare, Tier 4 only, forces player to respond (e.g. phone rings,
                 must pick up or stress spikes; door slams shut, path blocked)
    - The ParanormalManager knows which task the player is currently doing and
      selects events from that task's event pool (not a random global pool)
    - Each task has a field: List<ParanormalEventSO> ambientEventsWhileActive
    - Events are authored to feel spatially coherent with the task location
      (kitchen events happen near the kitchen, not the bathroom)
    - On Days 1–2, events are subtle enough that the player may question whether
      they imagined it. By Day 5+, they are unambiguous.

                          applies stress penalties at end of day for incomplete tasks
  [ ] AlarmClockManager   SetAlarm(), OnAlarmFired(), Snooze(), WakeUp()

  [ ] IInteractable       Interface: GetPromptText() + Interact()

  PHASE 3 — Apartment Scene (grey-box first, then dress)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Build interactables in this order:
  [ ] Computer            Two modes: Study Mode + Game Mode (Flappy Bird)
  [ ] Whiteboard          Renders today's TaskBoard data, shows checkmarks
  [ ] Alarm Clock         Set alarm interaction → AlarmClockManager.SetAlarm()
                          Wake-up minigames: Button Mash / Focus / Sequence
                          (rotate across days, escalate difficulty with stress)
  [ ] Bed / Sleep         Triggers end-of-day sequence → alarm set check →
                          advances DaySystem on successful wake-up
  [ ] Cat                 Walk near → "Feed Cat" → depletes food bowl object
  [ ] Cooking             Stove + fridge, timed interaction
  [ ] Laundry             Washer trigger → wait/skip → dryer
  [ ] Dishes              Sink interaction
  [ ] Garbage             Pick up bag, carry to door
  [ ] Workout             Floor area, button-press rhythm (E repeatedly or Q/E alt)

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PHASE 4 — Campus Scene
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [ ] SceneLoader         Preserves GameManager/StressManager across transitions
  [ ] Hallway             Long corridor, sparse NPCs, heavy ambient audio
  [ ] Lecture             Sit trigger → time-skip or "take notes" interaction
  [ ] Exam                Timed multiple-choice; harder if StressLevel is high
                          (options shuffle, timer shortens at high stress)

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  PHASE 5 — Paranormal System  (Mortuary Assistant style)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [ ] ParanormalManager   Listens to OnStressChanged + DaySystem + active task
                          Maintains ambient event pools per tier PER TASK
                          Fires events at weighted intervals without interrupting
                          current task; tracks "ignored event" count
  [ ] Task-Aware Events   Each task's SO holds its own ambient event list.
                          Events are spatially coherent with task location.
  [ ] Tier 1 Events       Monitor reflections, subsonic hum, faint footsteps
  [ ] Tier 2 Events       Doors, cat reaction, figure in hallway doorway,
                          stove light out, laundry machine self-starting
  [ ] Tier 3 Events       Your name spoken, shadow under door, phone buzz
                          (unknown contact), text scramble, browser tab opens
  [ ] Tier 4 Events       Blocking events (door slams shut, phone must be
                          answered), direct entity sighting, alarm minigame
                          horror injection on Days 5+
  [ ] Investigate/Ignore  Player can walk toward or away from any ambient event.
                          Ignoring too many escalates the Director's aggression.
                          Investigating costs task time → stress penalty risk.

  PHASE 6 — Socializing
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [ ] Discord Call UI     Full-screen overlay, avatar + name, dialogue options

  PHASE 7 — Polish  (Chilla's Art feel)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  [ ] Post-processing     Film grain, vignette, chromatic aberration

  [ ] Ambient Audio       Apartment hum, city outside, silence handled carefully
  [ ] Walk speed + FOV    Slow, slightly claustrophobic
  [ ] VHS / lo-fi look    URP post-process stack
  [ ] UI typography       Clean, minimal, slightly clinical

  HORROR DESIGN PRINCIPLES  (adapted from research & shipped horror games)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  These are engineering and design rules extracted from Frictional Games, Alien:
  Isolation, Amnesia post-mortems, and horror level-design analysis. Story
  specifics have been removed — everything below applies directly to this game.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1. SCARES ALONE DO NOT MAKE A HORROR GAME
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Horror should be judged by how well it fulfills its core principles, not by
  how generically "fun" it is. This game's horror is only as strong as the day
  loop underneath it. If the task/stress loop isn't satisfying to engage with,
  no amount of paranormal activity will save it.

  The three non-negotiable pillars for THIS game:

    MUNDANE PRESSURE       The daily routine feels real and genuinely demanding.
                           The player must actually care about completing tasks —
                           stress has to feel earned, not arbitrary.

    ANTICIPATION OVER      The paranormal works through imagination. A sound in
    SPECTACLE              the next room is scarier than a model in your face.
                           Space events apart. Silence is a tool.

    READABLE STAKES        The player always knows: what day it is, what tasks
                           remain, and roughly how stressed they are. Horror
                           needs a legible goal underneath the fear layer —
                           confusion is frustrating, not scary.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  2. THE DAY LOOP IS YOUR DESCENT LOOP
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Each day of Reading Week must take something away from the player:
    Mon–Tue  → Takes away TIME     (tasks pile up faster than expected)
    Wed–Thu  → Takes away SILENCE  (Tier 1–2 paranormal, apartment feels wrong)
    Fri–Sat  → Takes away SAFETY   (Tier 2–3 active, familiar spaces hostile)
    Sun      → Takes away SLEEP    (alarm minigame hardest, rest barely helps)
    Exam Mon → Takes away CONTROL  (Tier 3–4, stress floor non-negotiable)

  After each escalation, briefly restore a sense of safety before the next one.
  Recovery beats matter: finishing a chore, a successful Discord call, the cat
  behaving normally for a moment — these create contrast that makes the next
  scare land harder.

  Rule: space paranormal events apart. Constant shocks desensitize the player.
  One well-timed event after a long quiet stretch beats five cheap ones in a row.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  3. PROTOTYPE → VERTICAL SLICE → PRODUCTION  (do not skip steps)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  PROTOTYPE   Does the day loop feel worth playing?
              Minimum output: one full day (wake → tasks → sleep), stress meter
              working, one paranormal event firing, end-of-day penalty applying.
              Answer this before building the campus or any chore past #2.

  VERTICAL    Can you produce the game at the intended quality and pace?
  SLICE       Minimum output: Days 1–3, apartment fully grey-boxed, all chores
              stubbed, one polished paranormal encounter, final-feel audio mix,
              final-feel post-processing. Save/load working.

  PRODUCTION  Does the slice scale without new core tech?
              Reusable day configs (ScriptableObjects), paranormal event pool
              populated, campus scene built, all interactables production-ready.

  ALPHA       Systems locked. No new mechanics — only tuning, bug fixes,
              missing content.

  BETA        Content locked. Full playthrough, onboarding polish, performance
              pass, bug fixing.

  RULE: No new core systems after the vertical slice. If a new idea doesn't
  clearly strengthen Mundane Pressure, Anticipation, or Readable Stakes — cut
  it or save it for a future project.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  4. ARCHITECTURE: BOOTSTRAP SCENE + ADDITIVE LOADING
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Use a persistent Bootstrap scene that never unloads, plus additive scene
  loading for Apartment and Campus. This means:

    Bootstrap scene holds:  GameManager, DaySystem, StressManager,
                            TaskBoardManager, ParanormalManager,
                            AudioMixer, SaveSystem, global UI

    Loaded additively:      Apartment scene, Campus scene

  This is the only architecture that lets stress, day state, and paranormal
  logic persist cleanly across scene transitions without DontDestroyOnLoad hacks
  on every system.

  Keep content data in ScriptableObjects, not hardcoded in MonoBehaviours:
    - TaskData SO          one per day, defines task list + stress penalties
    - ParanormalEventSO    defines event type, tier, cooldown, conditions
    - AudioCueSetSO        groups audio clips by context (footsteps, ambient, etc.)

  Use ScriptableObject event channels for cross-system signals:
    OnStressChanged        → ParanormalManager, PostProcessingController, UI
    OnDayAdvanced          → TaskBoardManager, ParanormalManager, DayUI
    OnTaskCompleted        → StressManager, WhiteboardRenderer
    OnParanormalEventFired → AudioManager, LightingController

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  5. PARANORMAL ENTITY: DIRECTOR + LOCAL AI  (Alien: Isolation pattern)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Do NOT attempt a fully simulated omnipresent entity for v1. Use two layers:

    TENSION DIRECTOR       High-level. Knows current StressLevel, day number,
    (ParanormalManager)    time since last event, and player location. Decides
                           WHEN an event should fire and at what intensity.
                           Also handles pacing — if the player has been in a
                           high-stress state too long with no relief, it backs
                           off to allow recovery before escalating again.

    LOCAL EVENT            Low-level. The actual paranormal event playing out —
    (individual events)    a door opening, a sound cue, a figure appearing.
                           Each event is self-contained and fires when the
                           Director triggers it.

  This creates the illusion of a living, responsive haunting without simulating
  a full entity at all times. The Director is what makes it feel intelligent;
  the local events are what make it feel physical.

  Paranormal event states to implement (mirrors a FSM):
    Dormant → Activating → Active → Fading → Cooldown → Dormant

  IMPORTANT — Preview before pressure:
  Introduce each paranormal behavior in a safe/low-stress context before the
  player encounters it under real pressure. Example: on Day 1 a light flickers
  once while stress is at 10 — the player learns lights flicker. On Day 4 a
  light goes out entirely during a tense moment — they already have context.
  Never test a mechanic before teaching it.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  6. AUDIO IS YOUR PRIMARY HORROR TOOL  (not visuals)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Horror depends on anticipation and imagination. A sound that implies something
  is scarier than showing it. Design rule: the paranormal should be HEARD before
  it is SEEN, always.

  Systematize every audio layer:

    PLAYER FOOTSTEPS       Surface-aware (carpet vs. hardwood vs. tile).
                           Movement speed changes cadence. The player hears
                           themselves — silence when standing still matters.

    AMBIENT LAYER          Apartment hum, city outside, building settling.
                           This should never be fully absent. True silence = wrong.

    PARANORMAL AUDIO       Each tier has its own sound design language:
                           Tier 1: subsonic hum, barely-audible breathing
                           Tier 2: distant door creak, faint footsteps elsewhere
                           Tier 3: distinct sounds in adjacent rooms, voice fragments
                           Tier 4: immediate, close, unavoidable

    PROXIMITY HEARTBEAT    A low-frequency heartbeat layer that only activates
                           when StressLevel > 60. Keep it subtle — the moment it
                           becomes constant background noise it loses all meaning.

  Use Unity's Audio Mixer as the control room. Create named snapshots:
    "Calm"       → full ambience, no paranormal layer, normal EQ
    "Uneasy"     → ambience slightly ducked, paranormal layer at low volume
    "Active"     → ambience suppressed, paranormal prominent, slight reverb
    "Encounter"  → ambience muted, heartbeat in, LPF on everything else

  Transition between snapshots when StressLevel crosses tier thresholds.
  Do NOT hand-tune individual audio sources every time you rebalance tension.

  Noise propagation (for paranormal awareness, not an AI enemy):
  Emit a NoiseEvent (loudness, position) when the player makes noise — moving
  fast, dropping something, knocking. Attenuate by distance and wall count.
  Paranormal tier can react to this — activity slightly increases after the
  player makes noise above a threshold. Rewards calm, deliberate play.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  7. LEVEL LAYOUT: CONTROL KNOWLEDGE, NOT RANDOMNESS
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Fear comes from not knowing what's around a corner, not from random event
  placement. The apartment layout should use this deliberately:

  - The player should never be able to see the full apartment from one spot
  - Long sightlines (hallway to bathroom) exist specifically to be used for
    paranormal figures that vanish before the player reaches them
  - Every room needs a "safe corner" — somewhere the player can stand with
    their back to a wall. This makes paranormal events in open floor space
    feel more threatening by contrast.
  - Recovery spaces: the bathroom, inside the closet, under the desk.
    These aren't mechanics — they're psychological anchors. The player will
    seek them instinctively. Design paranormal events to respect and then
    violate those spaces as stress escalates.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  8. SAVE SYSTEM — KEEP IT SIMPLE
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Local JSON save via Application.persistentDataPath + JsonUtility.
  Save only what the player would expect to survive quitting:
    - Current day + time of day
    - StressLevel
    - Completed tasks (per day)
    - Key states (cat fed, laundry in machine, etc.)

  Do NOT try to serialize the full paranormal event state. On load, the
  ParanormalManager re-derives tier from StressLevel + day number — that's
  enough to restore the correct atmosphere without complex state serialization.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  9. TESTING & PERFORMANCE
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Use Unity Test Framework from the vertical slice onward. Cover:
    - Save/load round-trip (serialize → deserialize → state matches)
    - StressManager: penalties apply correctly at end-of-day
    - TaskBoardManager: correct tasks load per day
    - Paranormal event cooldowns: events don't double-fire

  Performance notes for this specific game:
    - Occlusion culling is essential. Room-and-corridor apartment layouts are
      exactly the geometry it was designed for. Enable it early, bake often.
    - Object pooling for paranormal event objects (particle effects, audio
      sources) — don't instantiate/destroy per event.
    - Profile on your actual target hardware, not just in the Editor.
      Audio Mixer DSP cost is invisible in the Editor but real on device.
